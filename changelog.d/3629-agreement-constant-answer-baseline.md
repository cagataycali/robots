### Fixed

- **episode_labels**: `measure_agreement` now reports the `quality_baseline` / `failure_mode_baseline` its two fractions have to beat - the score the best single CONSTANT answer earns on the same holdout, over exactly the episodes compared. The function exists to answer "should this judge be deciding what a policy trains on", and both fractions are accuracies over a column with a class balance, so the answer was unreadable off the number: a judge answering `quality="high", failure_mode=None` for every episode - one that read nothing - measured `quality_agreement 0.95` and `failure_mode_agreement 0.95` on a 20-episode holdout with a single tagged episode, and 1.0 on a holdout where nothing was tagged, with no field in the payload distinguishing it from a judge that looked. A recorded dataset is mostly clean, so that balance is the normal case rather than a contrived one, and unlike the two earlier corrections to this measurement (an unreadable grade in #2635, an out-of-vocabulary holdout tag in #2778) it needs no malformed input at all. Those two both moved a fraction in the direction that makes a SOUND judge look unsound; this is the other direction, where an unsound judge looks sound, which is the one that ends with a judge that cannot see filtering the training set. Measured on a real 16-episode two-camera MuJoCo recording with disjoint physically-induced ground truth, an open-weights VLM asked for the tag emitted `None` for all 16 episodes and scored `failure_mode_agreement 0.25` - exactly the base rate of the 4 untagged episodes, and exactly its baseline. The baseline is taken over the compared population rather than the whole holdout, because a holdout episode the judge never annotated is skipped by the fraction, so a baseline over the whole holdout would describe a set of episodes the fraction beside it never scored. Each baseline is `None` in step with the fraction it accompanies - a baseline over nothing is no more a measurement than an agreement over nothing.
