### Fixed

- **training/rl**: the two off-policy backends now preflight `tau`, the rate at which each target network tracks its online one, through a new `Trainer._polyak_coefficient_problems` gate on the half-open `(0, 1]` interval - one rule shared by both rather than a bare comparison duplicated in each. The field is spent in one expression per mirrored critic pair, `tp.mul_(1.0 - spec.tau).add_(spec.tau * p)`, so it decides whether a separate target network exists at all rather than merely how fast it moves, and its interval was the *precedent* the on-policy `gamma` and `lam` gates were written against: both cite "the sibling FastSAC preflight already bounds its own interval coefficient this way (`tau` must be in `(0, 1]`)" as the shape they generalize, while the check they cited was `if not 0.0 < spec.tau <= 1.0` in each backend. A bare comparison against the interval bounds left two holes those gates had already closed. `tau=True` was a silent `tau` of **one**, because `bool` is an `int` subclass - the maximum of the interval, at which the Polyak average degenerates to `tp.mul_(0.0).add_(p)` and the target network becomes a copy of the online network on every update. Measured on FastSAC over 200 timesteps with `validate()` returning `[]` and the run reporting `status="success"`: the largest online-to-target critic-parameter gap stayed at `0.0` exactly for all 19 Polyak updates, against `1.99e-04` rising to `3.92e-03` for the default `tau`, and a 60-timestep checkpoint was byte-identical to one from a run that asked for `tau=1.0`. So a flag landed as a request to switch target networks off, and nothing in the run said so. A numeric string, `None` or a list raised `TypeError: '<' not supported between instances of 'float' and 'str'` out of the comparison itself, from a `validate` documented to *return* its problems. `nan` and both infinities were already refused, but by an accident of Python's chained comparison rather than by a finiteness test; they stay refused, now with a reason that names finiteness, and `10**400` is refused for being outside the float64 range instead of having its 121 digits printed into the message. Neither endpoint changes reading, so nothing that worked becomes an error: `1.0` stays accepted as the deliberate hard update, and `0.0` stays refused because it freezes the target parameters at their initialization - the one decision that makes this interval half-open where the on-policy pair's is closed.
