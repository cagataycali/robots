### Fixed

- **episode_labels**: `measure_agreement` now holds a holdout entry's `failure_mode` to `FAILURE_MODES`, the taxonomy it compares that tag against. `FAILURE_MODES` is a field domain rather than one surface's convention - the docs state every field domain "holds in both directions - the writer refuses a value outside it, and so does the reader" - and the tag arrives in three spellings. Two were graded: the `failure_mode` argument of `annotate_episode`, and a `judge.failure_mode` loaded from the sidecar, so a judge's tag on disk is always `null` or a vocabulary member. The third, a holdout entry handed to `measure_agreement`, was the side of the comparison the domain had not reached. Because the judge's tag can only ever be `null` or a member, a holdout tag outside the vocabulary can never equal it, so it was counted as a disagreement: with the judge tagging `jerky_motion` and the human meaning the same thing, spelling it `"jerky"` took `failure_mode_agreement` from 1.0 to 0.0 and emitted a disagreement row naming the typo, under a successful call. Ten unusable spellings - a typo, a case variant, a stray trailing space, a hyphen, an empty string, an unknown label, an int, a bool, a list and a dict - all measured 0.0 the same way, and every one of them is refused by both of the other two spellings. The failure direction is one-way: a tag outside the vocabulary cannot match anything the judge is allowed to carry, so the reported agreement can only come out below the truth, which is the direction that makes a sound judge look unsound on the measurement whose stated purpose is deciding whether that judge should be filtering training data. The refusal names the holdout entry, the field, the vocabulary and the value, in the wording `annotate_episode` already uses for the same quantity, and it precedes the episode lookup so an entry the function cannot compare is refused whether or not that episode happens to carry an annotation - otherwise the refusal would depend on how much of the holdout the judge got through. An explicit `null` stays a real opinion ("no failure mode observed") and still compares, as do a holdout entry that carries no tag at all and every member of the taxonomy.
