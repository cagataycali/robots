### Fixed

- **tools/episode_judge**: `sample_frames` now refuses an episode whose frames are not all readable, instead of summarising the ones it could read. The tool reports `max_state_delta` ("for spotting discontinuities and teleports") and `rms_state_jerk` ("so a text-only judge can ground `jerky_motion` from state alone"), and both are computed over consecutive rows of the episode's frame series. The reader behind them walked `data/**/*.parquet` and skipped any shard it could not read, with a comment saying the skip mirrors `read_dataset_episode_indices`. It did not: that reader tolerates the same damage *and reports it*, returning every damaged file in `unreadable_files` and documenting its totals as a lower bound when the field is non-empty. `sample_frames` took the tolerance without the report, so the surviving rows reached the statistics as though they were consecutive. On a recording that moves a uniform 0.01 per step - no discontinuity anywhere - losing one middle shard of three took `max_state_delta` from 0.010 to 0.070 and `rms_state_jerk` from 0.000 to 6123.7, under `status="success"` with nothing naming the loss. That is fabricated evidence for exactly the failure modes the judge grades: `jerky_motion` and `drift` are both in the taxonomy, and `write_label` persists the judge's grade into the sidecar that filters training data, so a truncated download - "the usual outcome of an interrupted sync or hub download", in the sibling reader's own words - silently produced a wrong label on a good episode. It also showed as two tools disagreeing about one dataset: `load_episode` reported the true length while `sample_frames` reported the surviving count, both `success`. The refusal names every unreadable shard and how many of the episode's shards were damaged, and it precedes the empty-series check so a wholly unreadable episode reports the damage rather than "has no frames", which would name the wrong fault for frames that are present but unreadable. `load_episode` reads the episode metadata rather than the frames, so it still describes such an episode - including its true length. A parquet carrying no `episode_index` column is still skipped quietly: that is a non-frame table, not damage.
